> Choose the right structure, and the right technique, and a hard problem becomes an easy one.

This course is about choosing and building the right tool for a computational problem:

- data structures that stay fast however the data arrives (balanced trees, B-trees, tries);
- algorithms that search text and graphs efficiently;
- the main design techniques: divide and conquer, greedy, dynamic programming, backtracking, and branch and bound;
- the theory that tells you when no fast algorithm is likely to exist (NP-completeness).

Every topic page explains the idea with pictures first, then traces it step by step, then gives a short Python implementation you can run and change.

### Syllabus

- **Advanced data structures:**
  - binary search trees;
  - self-balancing trees: AVL, red-black and splay trees;
  - B-trees and B+ trees, segment trees and range trees;
  - suffix trees, N-ary trees, and tries and their types.
- **Pattern matching:** Knuth–Morris–Pratt, Boyer–Moore and Aho–Corasick.
- **Graph algorithms:**
  - DFS and BFS;
  - Dijkstra, Bellman–Ford and Floyd–Warshall;
  - minimum spanning trees by Prim's and Kruskal's algorithms.
- **Network flow:** the Ford–Fulkerson method, the Edmonds–Karp algorithm, and the max-flow min-cut theorem.
- **Divide and conquer:** merge sort, quicksort and closest pair.
- **Greedy:** coin change, activity selection, Borůvka's algorithm and Huffman coding.
- **Dynamic programming:** matrix-chain multiplication, longest common subsequence and 0/1 knapsack.
- **Backtracking:** N-Queens, Sudoku, graph colouring and Hamiltonian cycles.
- **Branch and bound:** 0/1 knapsack, and the travelling salesperson problem by FIFO and least-cost (LC) search.
- **Computational complexity:**
  - deterministic and nondeterministic algorithms;
  - the classes P and NP, NP-hard and NP-complete;
  - 3-SAT and the Cook–Levin theorem;
  - NP-hard graph problems: the clique decision problem (CDP) and the node cover decision problem (NCDP).
