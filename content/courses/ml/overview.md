> Machines learn from data. In this course you find out how, and build the programs yourself.

This course takes you through the main ways a computer can learn from experience:

- **decision trees**, which ask a series of questions;
- **Bayesian learning**, which reasons with probabilities;
- **neural networks and genetic algorithms**, which adjust numbers until the results improve;
- **rule learning and analytical learning**, which work with logic and prior knowledge;
- **reinforcement learning**, which learns from rewards.

Every topic page explains the idea in plain words, works through an example, and gives Python programs that **run right on the page**. Press **Run**, change something, and run it again. You don't need to install anything to start.

### Syllabus

- **Introduction:** well-posed learning problems; designing a learning system (training experience, target function, representation, approximation algorithm, final design); perspectives and issues in machine learning.
- **Decision tree learning:**
  - representation and appropriate problems;
  - the basic algorithm: choosing the best attribute, with an example;
  - hypothesis space search and inductive bias (restriction and preference biases, why prefer short hypotheses);
  - issues: overfitting, continuous values, alternative attribute measures, missing values and attribute costs.
- **Bayesian learning:**
  - Bayes theorem and concept learning, brute-force learning, MAP and consistent learners;
  - maximum likelihood and least-squared error, predicting probabilities, gradient search in a neural net;
  - minimum description length, Bayes optimal classifier and Gibbs algorithm;
  - the naive Bayes classifier and learning to classify text;
  - Bayesian belief networks: conditional independence, representation, inference and learning;
  - the EM algorithm: means of k Gaussians, the general statement, and deriving k-means.
- **Artificial neural networks:** representations, appropriate problems, perceptrons, multilayer networks and backpropagation, remarks, an illustrative example and advanced topics.
- **Genetic algorithms:** motivation, an illustrative example, hypothesis space search, genetic programming, and models of evolution and learning.
- **Learning sets of rules:** sequential covering, first-order rules, FOIL, induction as inverted deduction and inverting resolution.
- **Analytical and reinforcement learning:** inductive and analytical learning problems, learning with a perfect domain theory, and Q-learning.
